Explainable, Age-Aware Learning Analytics for Teachers

Research brief · Literature synthesis and position paper (2026)

Overview

Explainable AI in education almost always predicts and explains outcomes about students. This paper asks what happens when the teacher is the one being supported: could a system predict a teacher's own development, such as digital-skill growth, explain that prediction back to the teacher, and account for the teacher's age? I synthesized peer-reviewed work from 2021 to 2026 across three research threads to find out what already exists and where the gap lies.

Where the Gap Sits

The open gap: teacher as subject, age as parameter. Each thread is active on its own. What no study combines is a system that makes a prediction about a teacher's own practice, explains it to that teacher, and uses age as a parameter inside the model rather than a label reported beside it.

Key Findings

  1. Teacher AI adoption is a mature field. It is studied extensively with established technology-acceptance frameworks, so adoption alone is not an open question.
  2. Explainable AI targets students. SHAP dominates educational data mining and is used mainly to explain student performance, engagement and dropout. Even there, non-technical users find some explanation formats hard to read.
  3. Teachers act as validators, not subjects. Where teachers use XAI tools, they judge explanations about students. No reviewed system explains a prediction about the teacher's own development.
  4. Age is measured, not modeled. Age effects on adoption are real but small and point in different directions by outcome. Yet no study builds age into a predictive or adaptive model.
A precedent exists: recent trait-aware learning analytics work embeds personality as a moderator inside the model. This paper argues the same step can be taken for age, in a teacher-facing setting.

Approach and Limits

This is an informal, focused synthesis, not a formal systematic review. Sources were found through keyword searches on Google Scholar and the web, plus forward and backward citation chaining from central papers. There was no pre-registered protocol, dual screening or PRISMA quality scoring, so the paper is scoped for depth on one intersection rather than exhaustive coverage.

The gap it identifies motivates my planned doctoral research, and connects to my cognitive load and engagement project, which explains learner-data predictions with SHAP.
